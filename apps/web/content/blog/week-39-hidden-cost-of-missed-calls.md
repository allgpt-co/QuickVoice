# The Cost of Missed Calls: Measure the Gap Before Estimating Revenue

A missed call is evidence of an unanswered attempt. It is not automatically a lost customer or a full lifetime of revenue. Some attempts are spam, duplicates, existing-customer questions, or callers who successfully use another channel. A useful analysis identifies the actual service gap before attaching a value to it.

This guide supplies a worksheet for your own phone records. It makes no industry-wide claim about voicemail behavior or QuickVoice revenue recovery.

## Reconstruct what happened to each request

Choose a representative period and export the relevant phone-system records. Establish what that system means by missed, abandoned, voicemail, busy, and failed. Include call routing and staffed hours so you can distinguish an intentional voicemail path from a configuration fault.

Group attempts into likely requests using an approved, privacy-conscious method. A repeat caller trying several extensions should not automatically count as several new opportunities. Preserve uncertain matches rather than forcing them together.

| Record | What to inspect |
|---|---|
| Initial attempt | Time, line, route, and technical outcome |
| Later contact | Whether staff reached the person or another channel resolved the issue |
| Request category | New inquiry, existing service, administrative question, spam, or unknown |
| Final result | Completed service, delivered callback, declined offer, unresolved, or unobserved |
| Handling effort | Actual staff time and any correction/repeat work |

Do not fill unknown categories with an assumed conversion rate. If you cannot observe the final result, report it as unknown and make that limitation visible.

## Separate coverage gaps from value estimates

First report eligible requests that were not handled through any observed route. Break them down by hour, line, and request type. This can reveal a forwarding problem, an inaccurate published number, a peak-time staffing gap, or a need for clearer callback ownership.

Only then estimate potential value using your own comparable completed requests. Use contribution after direct service costs where the decision concerns financial benefit, and keep revenue, margin, capacity, and cash savings separate.

A scenario can be expressed as **eligible unresolved opportunities × assumed incremental completion rate × contribution per completed opportunity**. Label the assumptions and show a range. The incremental rate matters: some callers would complete their task later without the proposed change.

## Compare ways to close the observed gap

Options may include fixing routing, changing coverage, improving a staffed callback queue, publishing a clearer self-service answer, or testing a bounded AI intake workflow. Compare them against the same request population and full cost categories rather than assuming one technology is always cheaper.

For an AI pilot, define success as a verified outcome such as a correctly delivered callback request. Answering the line alone is not enough. Confirm provider capacity, unavailable-system behavior, and a route for callers who need a person.

## Evaluate the change without double counting

Use the same definitions before and after. Record seasonal differences, campaigns, staffing changes, and call mix. Link follow-up outcomes to the original requests so you do not count one person as both a newly captured lead and a separate recovered appointment.

Report unresolved requests, complaints, and manual work beside any improvement. Treat a before/after change as an observation with limits, not proof that every later sale was caused by the intervention.
